PhD dissertation

Essays on inclusive economic development: an ecosystem approach
Université Paris 8 · supervised by Thibault Darcillon and Matteo Cavallaro (Paris 8), Francesco Saraceno (Sciences Po/OFCE)

My dissertation develops a framework that conceptualizes regional economies as integrated ecosystems. Traditional models often miss the relationships between economic, social, and place-based factors. The ecosystem approach formalizes what practitioners see in their daily work, that interventions only work when everything connects. Three interconnected papers examine EU regional development through this lens: the first builds the framework, the second measures where regions stand, the third prices what the differences are worth in wellbeing.

Working paper Slides
Regional economic development as ecosystem: empirical evidence from EU regions
Paper 2 · working paper

Measures where European regions stand: they sort into a four-tier ladder along the three pillars, and the pandemic shock moved regions down that ladder four times as often as up.
The idea

Treats a region's economic, social, and place-based conditions as one interacting system and asks whether regions sort into coherent types, using Eurostat regional data. They do: four tiers, with most of the structure sitting between countries rather than within them. The pandemic shock was asymmetric, struggling regions kept their tier 92 percent of the time while high-performing regions kept theirs only 57 percent, and downward moves outnumbered upward four to one.

Working paper
Pricing Europe's regional ladder in wellbeing terms
Paper 3 · in progress

What is life on each rung of the regional ladder worth to a household, and how much of the gap between rungs is money versus the social fabric the income accounts miss? A calibrated model puts a number on both, and on the cost of sliding down.
The idea

Paper 2 measured the ladder, this paper prices it. Households in the model save against income risk and draw wellbeing from consumption and from the social fabric around them, living under their tier's conditions within their country's tax and transfer system. Comparing tiers gives the welfare gap in consumption-equivalent and wellbeing units, split into income, income risk, and the social component. The same numbers price the slide, the expected wellbeing cost of the pandemic-era transitions Paper 2 observed, and show how far national transfers already compress the ladder. The model values measured positions, it does not claim to explain them.

Where it stands

In progress. The calibration targets are built from Paper 2's regional panel, the model comes next.

Publications

Wellbeing and macroeconomics: a SAGE approach Univ. of Oxford, Social Macroeconomics WP series
Master's thesis · supervised by Xavier Ragot (Sciences Po/OFCE)

My master's thesis integrates social psychology into a standard macroeconomic model to better represent individual behavior during shocks. Moving beyond a simple consumption-leisure tradeoff, the model captures how households manage means and needs within economic systems, embedded in social networks and rooted in local environments.
