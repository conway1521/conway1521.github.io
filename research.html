PhD dissertation

Essays on inclusive economic development: an ecosystem approach
Université Paris 8 · supervised by Thibault Darcillon and Matteo Cavallaro (Paris 8), Francesco Saraceno (Sciences Po/OFCE)

My dissertation develops a framework that conceptualizes regional economies as integrated ecosystems. Traditional models often miss the relationships between economic, social, and place-based factors. The ecosystem approach formalizes what practitioners see in their daily work, that interventions only work when everything connects. Three interconnected papers examine EU regional development through this lens: place-based conditions, human and social capital, and economic activity.

Regional economic development as ecosystem: empirical evidence from EU regions
Paper 1 · draft forthcoming

Examines European regions as interconnected systems of conditions for inclusive development, testing whether regions sort into coherent clusters and what relationships hold between economic, social, and place-based indicators.
The idea

This paper is the empirical anchor of my disserattion, treating a region's economic, social, and place-based conditions as one interacting system and asks whether regions sort into coherent types along those conditions, using Eurostat regional data. It provides evidence that the theoretical model is built on.

A modular framework for inclusive economic development
Paper 2 · in progress

Builds a theoretical model of ecosystem dynamics through a modular structure that scales in complexity, and traces how a local shock propagates through interconnected economic, social, and place-based channels.
The idea

Formalizes the ecosystem as a modular structure, so complexity can be added one component at a time, and follows how a shock in one region travels through the economic, social, and place-based channels that connect it to the rest of the system.

Where it stands

In progress, following the empirical paper.

Evaluating EU cohesion policy through an ecosystem lens
Paper 3 · in progress

Applies the framework to public investment in regional development, separating direct effects from spillovers and identifying the conditions under which cohesion policy is most effective.
The idea

This paper turns the framework into an evaluation tool, separating the direct effect of a public investment from its spillovers across the ecosystem, and asks under what regional conditions cohesion spending actually moves inclusive development.

Where it stands

In progress, building on the modular framework.

Publications

Wellbeing and macroeconomics: a SAGE approach Univ. of Oxford, Social Macroeconomics WP series
Master's thesis · supervised by Xavier Ragot (Sciences Po/OFCE)

My master's thesis integrates social psychology into a standard macroeconomic model to better represent individual behavior during shocks. Moving beyond a simple consumption-leisure tradeoff, the model captures how households manage means and needs within economic systems, embedded in social networks and rooted in local environments.
