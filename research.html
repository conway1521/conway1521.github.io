Labour, place, and inclusive growth

Labour economics and regional economic development, with an emphasis on resilience, quality of life, and the systemic factors that shape inclusive growth.

PhD dissertation

Essays on inclusive economic development: an ecosystem approach
Université Paris 8 · supervised by Thibault Darcillon and Matteo Cavallaro (Paris 8), Francesco Saraceno (Sciences Po/OFCE)

The dissertation develops a framework that conceptualises regional economies as integrated ecosystems. Traditional models often miss the relationships between economic, social, and place-based factors. The ecosystem approach formalises what practitioners understand intuitively, that interventions only work when everything connects. Three interconnected papers examine EU regional development through this lens: place-based conditions, human and social capital, and economic activity.

Regional economic development as ecosystem: empirical evidence from EU regions
PhD paper 1 · draft forthcoming

Examines European regions as interconnected systems of conditions for inclusive development, testing whether regions sort into coherent clusters and what relationships hold between economic, social, and place-based indicators.
The idea

The empirical anchor for the ecosystem framing. It treats a region's economic, social, and place-based conditions as one interacting system and asks whether regions sort into coherent types along those conditions, using Eurostat regional data. It provides the evidence the theoretical model is built on.

A modular framework for inclusive economic development
PhD paper 2 · in progress

Builds a theoretical model of ecosystem dynamics through a modular structure that scales in complexity, and traces how a local shock propagates through interconnected economic, social, and place-based channels.
The idea

Formalises the ecosystem as a modular structure, so complexity can be added a component at a time, and follows how a shock in one region travels through the economic, social, and place-based channels that connect it to the rest of the system.

Evaluating EU cohesion policy through an ecosystem lens
PhD paper 3 · in progress

Applies the framework to public investment in regional development, separating direct effects from spillovers and identifying the conditions under which cohesion policy is most effective.
The idea

Turns the framework into an evaluation tool, separating the direct effect of a public investment from its spillovers across the ecosystem, and asking under what regional conditions cohesion spending actually moves inclusive development.

Fiscal policy's distributional impact on consumption
with Paul Hubert and Fergus Cumming · in progress

Identifies the channels through which fiscal policy reaches consumption across households of varying credit constraints, building on Hubert and Cumming (2021) to connect asset liquidity with a fiscal-policy dataset.
The idea

Asks how a fiscal shock reaches consumption differently depending on households' credit constraints and asset liquidity. It pairs a narrative identification of shocks with agent heterogeneity, so a fiscal shock's effect can be measured where it actually lands rather than on average.

Where it stands

In progress, with Paul Hubert and Fergus Cumming.

Measuring labour market resilience and AI exposure
calibrated task-skill framework · companion to the Skills work · in progress

Tasks are where labour supply meets demand, and where capital, AI included, substitutes for labour. A nested CES production function spans industry output, task composition, and skills, calibrated to accepted sources rather than re-estimated.
The idea

Most exposure measures score occupations directly. This reframes the market one level down, at the task, so that an occupation's exposure is built up from the tasks it performs and the skills those tasks require. The point is to operationalise what the literature already agrees on, with every parameter sourced.

The model

$$Y = A \cdot R^{\alpha} \cdot K^{\beta}$$

where the labour composite \( R = f(S) \) reads through a skills aggregator \( S = \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} \gamma_i \, s_i^{\,\rho}\right)^{1/\rho} \), with the elasticity of substitution between skills governed by \( \rho \), and \( K \) carrying the AI-substitution channel.

Where it stands

The production system is specified and running as an instrument. The paper writes up the calibration and the resilience results.

Wellbeing and macroeconomics: a SAGE approach Univ. of Oxford, Social Macroeconomics WP series
Master's thesis · supervised by Xavier Ragot (Sciences Po/OFCE)

Integrates social psychology into a standard macroeconomic model to better represent individual behaviour during shocks. Moving beyond a simple consumption-leisure tradeoff, the model captures how households manage means and needs within economic systems, embedded in social networks and rooted in local environments.
