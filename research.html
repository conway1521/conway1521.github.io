Research

My research focuses on labour economics and regional economic development, with a particular emphasis on resilience, quality of life, and the systemic factors that shape inclusive growth.

PhD dissertation

Essays on inclusive economic development: an ecosystem approach

Université Paris 8 · Supervised by Thibault Darcillon and Matteo Cavallaro (Paris 8), Francesco Saraceno (Sciences Po/OFCE)

My dissertation develops a novel framework conceptualising regional economies as integrated ecosystems. Traditional economic models often fail to capture the complex relationships between economic, social, and place-based factors. The ecosystem approach aims to formalise what practitioners understand intuitively: that interventions only work when everything connects. Its goal is to offer a modern, actionable framework for deepening economic analyses and a platform for policy interventions that can genuinely reduce inequalities and bolster resilience alongside prosperity.

The thesis comprises three interconnected papers examining EU regional development through this lens.

Working papers

Working paper PhD paper 1

Regional economic development as ecosystem: empirical evidence from EU regions

This paper examines European regions as interconnected systems comprising broad conditions for inclusive economic development. Using novel analytical frameworks and econometric techniques on Eurostat regional data, I investigate whether regional clusters emerge around shared characteristics and what relationships exist between economic, social, and place-based indicators. The analysis tests the ecosystem hypothesis and provides a data-driven basis for the theoretical model developed in subsequent papers.

In progress PhD paper 2

A modular framework for inclusive economic development

Building on empirical findings from Paper 1, this paper constructs a theoretical model capturing ecosystem dynamics of inclusive development. The framework bridges traditional economic thought with multifaceted realities through a modular structure allowing scalable complexity. The model explores how local shocks propagate through interconnected economic, social, and place-based channels.

In progress PhD paper 3

Evaluating EU cohesion policy through an ecosystem lens

This paper applies the theoretical framework to empirically evaluate the impact of public investments on regional development within the EU. Focusing on direct and spillover effects, it tests whether traditional investment strategies lead to equitable outcomes and identifies conditions under which Cohesion Policy interventions are most effective.

Working paper With Paul Hubert (Banque de France/Stanford) and Fergus Cumming

Fiscal policy's distributional impact on consumption

Fiscal policy is a significant lever for policymakers to influence the financial means of people. However, it can have drastically different implications given one's wealth and income situation. This project identifies the channels through which fiscal policy influences consumption among people of varying credit constraints through a novel method. Together with Paul Hubert and Fergus Cumming, we build upon their 2021 paper to connect individuals' asset liquidity with a unique fiscal policy dataset and estimate the specific consumption impacts. An alternative Narrative approach in conjunction with the heterogeneity of agents is employed, offering more accurate measurement of fiscal shock outcomes.

In progress Companion to the Skills Blueprint product line at Radius Intelligence

Measuring labour market resilience and AI exposure: a calibrated task-skill framework

Reframes the labour market at the task level: tasks are where supply meets demand, with capital (AI included) substituting for labour. A nested CES production function spans industry output, task composition, labour and capital aggregation, and the skills aggregation that makes up labour. The same engine reads top-down (industry shock to tasks to labour to skills) and bottom-up (skills to roles to tasks to output). Resilience emerges as a measurable quantity: a skill is exposed when it is heavily used in tasks that are large in output and becoming more capital-intensive. Every parameter is calibrated to accepted sources in the task and skills literature rather than re-estimated; the paper operationalises what the literature agrees on rather than contesting it.

Wellbeing and macroeconomics: a SAGE approach

Master's Thesis · Supervised by Xavier Ragot (Sciences Po/OFCE)

This thesis integrated social psychology concepts into a standard macroeconomic model to better represent individual behaviour during economic shocks. By moving beyond simple consumption-leisure tradeoffs, the model captures how households manage means and needs within economic systems, embedded in social networks and rooted in local environments.
