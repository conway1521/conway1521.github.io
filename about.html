About

Regional development policy is not working. Despite decades of investment, inequality persists and the communities most in need are often left further behind. My work asks why and builds what comes next.

The problem does not seem to be lack of effort or funding. It is that we have treated economic development as a collection of isolated interventions when in reality, regional economies are ecosystems. Labour markets, housing, transport, education, employer needs, and social networks all interact. Policies that ignore these connections do not just underperform; they often make things worse.

My PhD at Université Paris 8 formalises this insight into a new framework, OIKOS — named for the Greek word for household, the root of both "economy" and "ecology", as a reminder that the goal of economics is to improve people's standing in society (read more). The framework examines how place-based conditions, human and social capital, and economic activity interact across EU regions. The goal is not just academic: it is to understand why well-intentioned policies fail, so we can design ones that do not.

At Radius Intelligence, I am building this thinking into practice. As Head of Data Science, I design workforce planning infrastructure that combines real-time labour market data, rigorous analytical methods, and economic intelligence into something policymakers, employers, training providers, and workforce boards can actually use. Not dashboards that sit on shelves, but a living system that helps regions anticipate shortages, match people to opportunities, and build resilience before shocks hit.

Previously, I led workforce and economic insights at Jobs for the Future, developing a national green workforce readiness dashboard, advising Verizon's initiative to place 500,000 under-represented individuals into quality careers, and analysing how AI is transforming jobs. My master's thesis at Sciences Po (Fulbright Scholar) integrated social psychology into macroeconomic models to explore how personal wellbeing shapes economic decisions (published in the University of Oxford Social Macroeconomics series).
