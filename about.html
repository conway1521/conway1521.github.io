About

Why regional policy fails, and what comes next

Regional development policy is not working. Despite decades of investment, inequality persists and the communities most in need are often left further behind. My work asks why, and builds what comes next.

The problem does not seem to be effort or funding. It is that we have treated economic development as a collection of isolated interventions when regional economies are ecosystems. Labour markets, housing, transport, education, employer needs, and social networks all interact. Policies that ignore these connections do not just underperform; they often make things worse.

My PhD at Université Paris 8 formalises this insight into a framework, examining how place-based conditions, human and social capital, and economic activity interact across EU regions. The goal is not only academic: it is to understand why well-intentioned policies fail, so we can design ones that do not.

At Radius Intelligence I put this thinking into practice, the ecosystem framing built end to end and operational. As Head of data science and engineering I run the department across economics, engineering, and data science. Employer forecasting and workforce planning on one side, policy and labour market analysis on the other, and a data substrate that stands as a product in its own right, covering every US occupation at every depth of geography.

Previously I led workforce and economic insights at Jobs for the Future, developing a national green-workforce readiness dashboard, advising a 500,000-person placement initiative, and analysing how AI is transforming jobs. My master's thesis at Sciences Po (Fulbright scholar) integrated social psychology into macroeconomic models, published in the University of Oxford Social Macroeconomics series.
