Open Inequality Atlas: wealthLive · updated continuously

Authoritative wealth-inequality data is scattered across mutually incompatible sources: WID, the ECB's HFCS, the Luxembourg Wealth Study, the US SCF, and the Fed's Distributional Financial Accounts. Each uses a different unit, methodology, and licence, so a comparable cross-country panel is a research project in itself. The Atlas closes that gap into a single, versioned, openly licensed panel of household net-wealth inequality, 10,783 observations across 213 countries (1800-2025), every row tagged by source priority and comparability tier.

Labour market digital twinIn development

Analyses how labour markets interact with training providers, adjacent workforces, and migration, visualising steady-state dynamics and how shocks propagate. Combines micro-to-macro reasoning with granular workforce data to train a realistic model of local and state labour markets.

Skills-based labour marketIn development

Views labour markets as combinations of skill blueprints feeding the roles that meet demand. Rather than treating labour as a homogeneous input, the production function is reformulated as:

$$Y = A \cdot R^{\alpha} \cdot K^{\beta}$$

where \( R = f(S) \) is a skills composite and \( S = \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} \gamma_i \, s_i^{\,\rho}\right)^{1/\rho} \) aggregates individual skills \( s_i \) with elasticity of substitution governed by \( \rho \).

This view connects credential-based learning and training to opportunities for upskilling and for tapping adjacent workforces to fill gaps, and lets us analyse shifts like AI on how roles evolve and which skills are more resilient.
