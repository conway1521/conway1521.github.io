Open Inequality Atlas: wealth

Authoritative wealth-inequality data is scattered across mutually incompatible sources: WID, the ECB's HFCS, the Luxembourg Wealth Study, the US SCF, and the Fed's Distributional Financial Accounts. Each uses a different unit, methodology, and licence, so a comparable cross-country panel is a research project in itself. The Atlas closes that gap into a single, versioned, openly licensed panel of household net-wealth inequality: 10,783 observations across 213 countries from 1800 to 2025, every row tagged by source priority and comparability tier.

The Skills DNA of the economy
task-based labour market, calibrated AI exposure · in progress

Roles, tasks, and skills are three equivalent decompositions of the same output. Price every task and every skill, and AI exposure becomes a question of dollars: which steps an appliance can do, which abilities lose their market, and who is affected.
The idea

Push occupational pay down through the tasks each role performs and the skills each task demands. Nothing is created or destroyed, so the same national total appears as industry output, occupational pay, the value of tasks, and the value of skills. AI enters as an appliance that can perform some priced steps, which turns exposure into a measurable flow of dollars.

The model

$$Y = A \cdot R^{\alpha} \, K^{\beta}, \qquad R = \left(\sum_{k} \gamma_k \, s_k^{\,\rho}\right)^{1/\rho}$$

In levels the identity is theory-free arithmetic. The task shares \( \beta_i \), skill shares \( \gamma_{ik} \), and automatable shares \( \delta_i \) are read straight from the data. The CES curvature and augmentation \( A \) enter only for counterfactuals.

The workforce flow engine
occupational flow parameters · in progress

Every occupation in every state has a metabolism: who enters, who exits, who moves, and a background rate of churn. Standard forecasts give the totals. This measures the rates underneath them from Census microdata, so policy can act on the causes of a workforce change.
The idea

Standard forecasts answer how many. This decomposes an occupation's stock into entry, exit, migration, and residual churn at state by occupation by age band by year. The churn residual is kept as a behavioral parameter, so a state can tell a retention problem from a pipeline problem.

The model

$$\text{Stock}_{t+1} = \text{Stock}_{t} + \text{Entrants} + \text{In-migrants} - \text{Exits} - \text{Out-migrants} + \text{Residual churn}$$

A cohort-component identity on ACS repeated cross-sections. Migration is observed directly. Entry and exit are estimated from age-band dynamics and validated against external records.

Does the choice of AI-exposure measure matter?
common task framework, AI exposure · in progress

At least six measures score how exposed each occupation is to AI, and studies pick one without justifying it. If they disagree, the ranking of at-risk jobs is a property of the instrument. This holds everything constant except the measure.
The idea

Each measure is a swappable adapter over one shared occupation and skill vocabulary, so the identical downstream computation can run under each and report exactly where, and how much, the choice changes the answer.

The approach

Hold the engine fixed, swap the exposure adapter, and measure agreement: rank correlations, the share of employment whose exposed-or-not classification flips, and whether the disagreement lands in the policy-relevant middle.

What does a vacancy measure?
labour market measurement · in progress

Open jobs sound like one number but are three objects: what employers tell a survey, what job boards advertise, and what an applicant tracking system records. For the same employers they disagree by two to three times. This reconciles them.
The idea

Using requisition lifecycle records from an employer panel, document what an applicant-tracking ledger actually contains, compute true time-to-fill by occupation, and decompose the two-to-three-times gap into named parts.

The approach

A concept map from each requisition state to its survey and posting counterpart, Kaplan-Meier durations that keep still-open requisitions instead of dropping them, and a stale-inventory diagnostic that surfaces requisitions left open without being worked.

Shortage arithmetic
occupational gaps, substitution · in progress

Headline shortages subtract the supply of one occupation from the demand for that same occupation. But demand is for output, and employers substitute across near occupations. Ignoring that margin overstates shortages by 15 to 20 percent.
The idea

Take any headline gap, find the substitution-feasible adjacent occupations by skill similarity, bound how much each adjacency tier can absorb, and count only workers who clear skill, availability, licensing, and wage screens. Republish the gap as a range.

The model

$$\text{gap}_{\text{corrected}} = \text{gap}_{\text{raw}} - \sum_{\text{tiers}} \min\!\big(\text{absorbable share} \times \text{adjacent slack},\ \text{tier capacity}\big)$$

Reading the Beige Book with LLMs
text as data, labour sentiment · in progress

The twelve Federal Reserve districts publish qualitative labour-market reports eight times a year. An LLM extracts structured sentiment from each release for about thirteen cents, so forty years of text can be processed for the price of lunch.
The idea

Extract a fixed schema per district, labour tightness, wage pressure, sector mentions, and confidence, across the full archive, release it as a public dataset, and validate it against hand-coded samples, the older dictionary indices, and hard district data.

The approach

The cost is part of the argument. At thirteen cents a release the economics of text-as-data pipelines change, which is what makes the full historical run, and the public series it produces, feasible at all.

Fiscal policy's distributional impact on consumption
with Paul Hubert and Fergus Cumming · in progress

Identifies the channels through which fiscal policy reaches consumption across households of varying credit constraints, building on Hubert and Cumming (2021) to connect asset liquidity with a fiscal-policy dataset.
The idea

Asks how a fiscal shock reaches consumption differently depending on households' credit constraints and asset liquidity. It pairs a narrative identification of shocks with agent heterogeneity, so a fiscal shock's effect can be measured where it actually lands rather than on average.
