Data science / ML Engineering

The occupation resolver

Any messy employer job title, resolved to a federal occupation code and a recruiter-legible canonical name, with certainty spent cheapest first.

A ten-stage cascade in front of three model layers: an open-source embedding baseline, a fine-tuned in-domain encoder, and a local LLM for the long tail. Nothing passes below its confidence floor; the unmatched are flagged, never guessed.

Economics

Labor supply as flows

Projecting a state's workforce as a conserved system: five inflows feed the stock, three outflows drain it, demand is built four ways, and the gap is what the engine solves for.

Cohort accounting over 501 occupations and 51 jurisdictions, on nineteen vintages of migration microdata. The accounting identity must hold, so every term is measured honestly, raw 19% termination becomes a true 4.29% exit once transfers are removed.

Policy Economics

Where opportunity is, and what moves it

Scoring every occupation on five transparent pillars, then attaching only the policy levers that have evidence behind them, always with a confidence band, never a bare point.

The Job Opportunity Index keeps its pillars visible so the score is never a black box; levers carry their literature basis and an evidence split into causal, quasi-experimental, and observational studies.
