Radius Intelligence (RI)

Labour market intelligence, end to end

May 2025 - Present · San Francisco · Head of data science and engineering
Current

Radius is a two-tier labour market intelligence platform that bridges labour supply and demand through an intelligent data layer, computational engines, and analytical lenses. I built and run the data department end to end, taking the ecosystem framing from my dissertation into practice.

RI Technologies handles client-side employer workforce planning: HRIS standardisation, vacancy and fill-rate diagnostics, turnover and demand forecasting with scenario modelling, and prescriptive guidance through training and staffing inventories. An AI analyst agent, Eclipse, runs across the workflow.

RI Labs handles labour market and policy analysis for regional stakeholders: occupation scoring on demand, mobility, growth, automation durability, and job quality; skill-based transition pathways; forward-looking market signals across every US state and occupation; and synthesis reports on regional conditions, sector health, and supply-demand imbalances.

Both run on a compliant multi-tenant Supabase data layer that reconciles federal statistics, commercial sources, training outcomes, employer HRIS, federal spending, and academic data across every US occupation and geography, standardised through a SOC pipeline of regex, embedding matching, and LLM fallback, with transformer fine-tuning for occupational and skills intelligence. Underpinned by flow-based labour market economics: stock-flow decomposition, search and matching frictions, cohort projection, micro-to-macro. Explicit econometrics over black-box ML.

Engines

Workforce flow engine (PWFE)

Active

Cohort-based supply modelling at state and occupation level, decomposing year-over-year workforce stock into graduates, exits, and net migration. First end-to-end run: NC registered-nurse supply 109,652 in 2024 to 138,494 by 2033, on 19 ACS PUMS vintages across 501 occupations and 51 jurisdictions.

Demand engine (PWDE)

Active

Demand-side triangulation across three non-reconciled layers, where divergence is the signal: pooled employer requisition rates, structural anchors from BLS projections and OEWS, and population-anchored forward-need scenarios with aging adjustment. Live v0.8 across 18 healthcare occupations in NC.

Lenses

Opportunity scoring (JOI)

Active

Five-pillar Job Opportunity Index across 250-plus occupations: demand, mobility, growth, automation durability, and job quality. NC production run scored 227 occupations, range 0.30 to 0.72, with 98-plus percent rank stability under perturbation.

Skill-similarity mapping (SPI)

Active

798,000 occupation-pair similarities derived from O*NET skills, knowledge, and work activities. Backs transition pathways and the adjacent-workforce playbooks.

Signals

Active

Forward-looking market signals across 50 states, 11 to 15 signal types over two layers, from wage location quotients and retirement pressure to skill drift, AI exposure, WARN notices, and Beige Book sentiment.

Skills DNA

In development

Reframes the labour market at the task level, with skills as the production input and AI as substituting capital. A three-level nested CES production function, fully calibrated rather than estimated. Engine v1 built, product phases pending. Companion paper in progress.

Supply-demand ratio

Levers

Architecture

Layered data model

Active

Raw, cache, reference, and lake tiers, with a central catalog registry that makes every dataset discoverable and versioned. Every row tagged on six axes: readiness, lifecycle, geo grain, time grain, temporal type, and an answers-concepts vocabulary.

Compliant tenant isolation

Active

Row-level security so every client sees only their own data while the shared reference and lake layers stay common. Only opaque mappings and learnings flow to the shared layer.

NC nursing workforce simulation

A comprehensive simulation projecting nursing supply across all 100 North Carolina counties through 2035. Integrates licensure data, employer HRIS records from key health systems, granular training-pipeline data from every provider in the state, and adjacent populations to estimate facility-level shortages, geographic distributions, and talent mismatches. The workforce flow engine's first end-to-end run took RN supply from 109,652 in 2024 to a projected 138,494 by 2033.
