The work

The occupation resolver

Any messy employer job title resolved to a federal occupation code and a recruiter-legible canonical name, with certainty spent cheapest first.

A ten-stage cascade in front of three model layers: an open-source embedding baseline, a fine-tuned in-domain encoder, and a local LLM for the long tail. No ground truth to start from, so the training set is generated and reviewed at scale. Nothing passes below its confidence floor; the unmatched are flagged, never guessed.

The employer digital twin

One employer's raw HR export, taken from upload to forecast: standardise, extract a canonical panel, explore with seasonality, then project and cost the scenario levers.

An end-to-end product where each pipeline stage prints what it produced. Survival models and cohort accounting on the analytics side, a benchmark panel built under k-anonymity, and a scenario layer that puts a defensible number and a confidence band on each intervention.

The labour market digital twin

A state's workforce as a conserved system: five inflows feed the stock, three outflows drain it, demand is built four ways, and the gap is what the model solves for.

Cohort accounting over 501 occupations and 51 jurisdictions on nineteen vintages of migration microdata, with an opportunity index, forward-looking signals, and evidence-graded policy levers on top. The accounting identity must hold, so every term is measured honestly, raw 19% termination becomes a true 4.29% exit once transfers are removed.

The data and standardization substrate

The lake everything stands on: 220 datasets across twelve domains, reconciled onto one occupational spine, with standardisation treated as a first-class product.

Federal, commercial, and academic sources joined with anonymised employer contributions, every dataset tagged for readiness, grain, and provenance, and kept honest by automated drift checks. The engineering the rest of the portfolio stands on.
