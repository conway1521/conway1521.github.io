Portfolio

Selected projects

Components of the workforce intelligence platform I built at Radius, described in broad terms. The two digital twins are the ones to start with, the employer's and the state's. The rest are smaller problems I had to solve to get the twins working: not data science for its own sake, each one makes a longer chain possible. All are solved end to end, drawing on economics, machine learning, engineering, and product. The code and the underlying data stay private (owned by Radius Intelligence), what each page shows is just the problem I solved, how the approach works, and where it landed. Every figure is built on synthetic data for illustration only.

Digital twins

Employer digital twinAn employer wants to know: is our turnover bad, what is coming, what should we do about it. Their HR export answers none of that on its own, so the twin takes it from upload to forecast and scenarios, every number auditable back to the raw rows. End to end console Data science · engineering · econometrics · product Labor market digital twinStates plan their workforce with statistics that lag by years and projections that arrive as black boxes. The twin models the market as stocks and flows that must balance: who enters, who leaves, demand built four ways, and the gap left over. Flow model · supply-demand divergence Economics · policy · data science

Components

Occupation resolverEmployers name the same job a hundred ways, and nothing downstream works until those titles collapse onto one taxonomy: no benchmark, no forecast, no comparison across employers. The resolver fixes this once, for any title, which is why the twins can exist. Resolver · ten-stage pipeline · three model layers Machine learning · LLM systems · data engineering Data lakeConnecting datasets across the labor market takes a crosswalk onto the federal taxonomy, and the sources disagree on unit, grain, vintage, and license. The lake reconciles 220 of them so any one can be joined to any other. Lake · canon · librarian Data engineering · data science Workforce blueprintsA new facility is planned and a region needs to know what it means: how many workers, in which occupations, on what schedule. Announcements give a headline headcount and staffing statistics describe yesterday's mix, so the blueprint has to be built. Blueprints per 100 workers · derivation table Economics · data science Occupational opportunity scoringA workforce board has one real question: which occupations should we back, and what would actually move them. This scores every occupation on five visible pillars and attaches levers only where the evidence supports them, with a confidence band, never a bare point. Index · levers with intervals · observatories Policy · economics Task-based labor marketThe main measures of AI exposure disagree with each other, and claims about AI's effect on jobs outrun anyone's ability to measure them. This is an instrument rather than another estimate: a calibrated production function where every parameter is disclosed, sourced, and swappable. Nested framework · parameter provenance Economics · machine learning Market signalsOfficial statistics arrive quarters late and decisions cannot wait for them. This grades fifteen kinds of leading evidence, postings, layoff notices, wage anomalies, by how much confidence each deserves, and composes them into two flags whose logic stays readable. Evidence types · two flags Economics · data science
