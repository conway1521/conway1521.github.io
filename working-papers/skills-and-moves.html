Do skills predict moves?

A whole industry now advises workers and boards using skill-similarity scores: these two occupations are 98.5 percent similar, so a machinist can retrain into industrial maintenance in a few months. But similarity is a hypothesis about feasible moves. This tests it against the moves workers actually make.

Economics · data science. A gravity model of occupational mobility on public microdata.
01

The idea

Skill-similarity scores come from occupational skill, knowledge, and activity profiles, and they are used as if a high score were evidence that people move, when what a high score actually claims is that a move is feasible, which is a different thing. The test, run at scale, is simple to state: do workers move between occupations in proportion to their skill similarity, once you hold size, wages, and geography constant?

02

The model

A gravity model of occupational flows, with the observed flow from occupation \( i \) to \( j \) written against similarity, the wage gap, the two employment sizes, and a licensing barrier at the destination:

$$\ln \text{flow}_{i \to j} = \eta \, \ln \text{sim}_{ij} + \beta_1 \ln \tfrac{w_j}{w_i} + \beta_2 \ln E_i + \beta_3 \ln E_j + \gamma \, \text{licensed}_j + \varepsilon_{ij}$$

The headline is \( \eta \), the elasticity of transitions to skill similarity, estimated with a method that keeps the many pairs where no one moves rather than dropping them.

03

What it settles

01
The number retraining advice assumes.
The similarity elasticity is the coefficient every skill-based recommendation implies without stating. Estimating it says how much of a real move a point of similarity actually buys.
02
Whether the tier thresholds are right.
The field treats fixed cutoffs as the line between an easy move and a hard one. Fitting a flexible curve of similarity against observed movement shows where the flows actually break, and whether the assumed thresholds match or need recalibrating.
03
Where similarity is not enough.
A residual map of pairs that move far more or far less than similarity predicts locates the frictions similarity misses: licensing walls, geographic mismatch, and information gaps. Those residuals are as informative as the fit.
04

What is next

The one new build is the observed transition matrix, constructed from matched Census records with the standard corrections for occupation-coding noise, which otherwise invents moves that never happened and is where this kind of study usually fails. With the matrix in hand the model estimates directly and the tiers calibrate. The payoff reaches beyond this paper: the estimated thresholds replace the assumed ones in the shortage-arithmetic correction, which currently takes those cutoffs as given.
