The employer digital twin

One employer's raw HR export, taken from upload to forecast: standardize, extract a canonical panel, explore with seasonality, map, then project and cost the scenario levers. Every phase prints what it produced.

Data science · engineering · applied econometrics · product. In production on employer panels, under tenant isolation.
01

The problem

An employer wants three answers: is our turnover bad, what is coming, and what should we do about it. Their HR export answers none of them: hundreds of column variants, freetext titles, termination codes that differ by system, and transfers recorded as if people had quit. Worse, any answer has to survive a sceptical CFO: it must be comparable to peers and auditable back to the raw rows.

02

How it works

01Ingest anything

Headcount, requisitions, hires, terminations, and contingent workers arrive in whatever shape the employer's system exports. Columns map by fuzzy match with confidence scores. Formats, types, and outliers are cleaned and flagged, not silently fixed.

Data engineering · product

02Standardize to a canonical panel

Titles resolve to federal occupation codes through the occupation resolver, termination reasons and requisition statuses map to controlled vocabularies, facilities geocode. Transfers are separated from real exits: that single correction moves a raw 19% termination rate to a true 4.29% and changes every projection built on it.

Data science · economics

03Measure under floors

Survival curves and cohort retention for who stays and who leaves. Seasonal decomposition to separate recurring patterns from real trends. Peer benchmarks built under k-anonymity with suppression floors, so a small cell is withheld rather than shown noisy.

Econometrics · data science

04Project, then intervene

The forecasting method is chosen by data depth, never by default, and every projection carries its confidence band. Market conditions are decomposed from employer-specific effects, and each candidate intervention arrives with a literature-derived range and a realistic capture ceiling.

The accounting identity stocks and flows

Supply is a bathtub. Every projection has to satisfy the identity, so every term has to be measured.

stockt+1 = stockt + entrants − exits
+ inflow − outflow
Raw HRIS termination rate19.0%

70 to 80% of recorded terminations are internal transfers. Correcting one term changes the projection by an order of magnitude.

04

Final state

The pipeline runs in production on employer panels: the same stages the console shows, over real headcount, requisition, hire, and termination data, behind row-level security. The run above is synthetic so it can be shown at all. Real runs, and the peer benchmarks they feed, stay under tenant isolation. Every figure a report shows is traceable to the pipeline stage and the raw rows that produced it. Where outputs can be checked against published benchmarks they are: modeled time-to-close for high-volume clinical roles matches the national benchmark for comparable positions to the day.
