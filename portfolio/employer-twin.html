Employer digital twin

An employer wants to know: is our turnover bad, what is coming, what should we do about it. The twin takes their raw HR export from upload to forecast and scenarios, and keeps every number auditable back to the raw rows.

Data science · engineering · applied econometrics · product. In production on employer panels, under tenant isolation.
01

The problem

An employer wants to answer questions like: is our turnover bad, what is coming, and what should we do about it. However, their HR export answers none of them: hundreds of column variants, freetext titles, termination codes that differ by system, and transfers recorded as if people had quit. In order to convince CFOs and the boardroom, answers must be both comparable to peers, integrated with other datasets, and auditable back to the raw rows.

02

How it works

01Ingest anything

Headcount, requisitions, hires, terminations, and contingent workers arrive in whatever shape the employer's system exports (which can differ for the same employer over time). Columns map by fuzzy match with confidence scores. Formats, types, and outliers are cleaned and flagged.

Data engineering · product

02Standardize to a canonical panel

Titles resolve to federal occupation codes through the occupation resolver, termination reasons and requisition statuses map to controlled vocabularies, facilities geocode. Transfers are separated from real exits, with canonical reasons attributed. Clean separations of contingent and full time employers.

Data science · economics

03Status quo analysis

Survival curves and cohort retention for who stays and who leaves, seasonal decomposition to separate recurring patterns from real trends. Comparison to anonymous peer benchmarks for contextualisation.

Econometrics · data science

04Project, then intervene

Forecasting methods are chosen by data depth, and every projection carries its confidence band. Market conditions are decomposed from employer-specific effects, and each candidate intervention has a literature-derived range.

04

Final state

The pipeline runs in production on employer panels: the same stages the console shows, over real headcount, requisition, hire, and termination data, behind row-level security (the run above is fully synthetic to share it). Outputs are intended to be checked against published benchmarks, such as modeled time-to-close for high-volume clinical roles matches the national benchmark for comparable positions to the day.
