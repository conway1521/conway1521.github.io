The labour market digital twin

A state's workforce as a conserved system: five inflows feed the stock, three outflows drain it, demand is built four ways, and the gap is what the model solves for.

Economics · policy · data science · engineering — first end-to-end run projecting a state nursing workforce to 2033, informing state policy
01

The problem

States plan their workforce with statistics that lag by years and live in silos: employment here, graduations there, migration somewhere else, none of them in the same accounting frame. Projections arrive as black boxes, a number with no visible mechanics. The alternative is to model the market the way an economist would audit it: as stocks and flows that must balance, with every term measured, sourced, and honest about its uncertainty.

02

How it works

01Supply as conserved flows

Cohort accounting over 501 occupations and 51 jurisdictions: graduates in from the training pipeline, migration in and out from nineteen vintages of census microdata, exits and retirements from empirical age-specific rates. The identity must hold, so nothing can be fudged.

Economics · data science

02Demand built four ways, never averaged

Requisition signals, structural projections, population-anchored forward need, and facility blueprints, four estimates of the same demand, deliberately kept apart. Where they agree, act; where they diverge, investigate. Divergence is information, not error.

Economics · data science

03Every term measured honestly

Transfers are removed from exits before anything is projected. Thin cells fall back to national rates and say so. Harmonising two decades of occupational codes carries uncertainty flags rather than silent joins.

Data science · engineering

04From projection to decision

On top of the engine: transition pathways gated by credential requirements and wage premiums, forward-looking market signals, and evidence-graded policy levers, so a projected gap comes with the moves that could close it.

03

What's real

The structure is the production system: the same flows, identities, and demand layers run over real census microdata, training-pipeline records, and employer panels. The first end-to-end run projects one state's nursing workforce through 2033 across all of its counties, and is informing state workforce policy and training investment. The curves and magnitudes shown here are illustrative; the real runs carry their sources and uncertainty with them.
