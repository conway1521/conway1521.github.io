Data lake

A lake everything else in this portfolio stands on: 220 datasets across twelve domains, reconciled onto one federal occupational crosswalk, with standardization treated as a first-class product.

Data engineering · data science. 220 datasets · dozens of providers · over a million contributors · millions of records.
01

The problem

In order to connect relevant datasets across relevant sources spanning the entire labor market, a crosswalk onto the federal taxonomy is necessary. Federal statistics, commercial postings, training records, credential rosters, and employer files however do not match in unit, grain, vintage, and license, nor do they name an occupation the same way. Worse yet, the most valuable data, employers', can only join this pool if anonymity is engineered into the system.

02

How it works

01Crosswalk map

Crosswalks through a federal taxonomy bring industry, program, and skill classifications together. Controlled vocabularies and a specifically defined Canon, the naming and calculation conventions I wrote, standardize everything ambiguous (like termination reasons, employment status, facility types) so tenant data and public data match up.

Data engineering · data science

02Registry as a product

Every dataset carries tags for readiness, lifecycle stage, geographic and time grain, and provenance class. The catalog is queryable, versioned, and the single source of truth for what exists, at what quality, made from what. It also serves as the consistent foundation for future work.

Data engineering · product

03Contribution without exposure

Employer data joins under k-anonymity floors and row-level tenant isolation, with opaque identifiers throughout. The detailed panel is the source of truth with benchmarks as aggregated views over it.

Data engineering · privacy

04Constantly checked

Documentation drift is checked on every change through counts that regenerate daily and commit themselves. Any phantom or orphaned table gets audited automatically so that the registry cannot diverge from the database it describes.

Canon one metric, set in full

I wrote the Canon in order to have a naming and calculation convention for the whole system to follow. This way, anything ingested is standardized to it, and a metric is only shown with its definition, and source attached.

vacancy rate = open ÷ (headcount + open)
measured from
Employer panel, requisition lifecycle rows, monthly grain
standardized by
Title resolution to the federal crosswalk, level bands, termination decomposition
suppressed when
Headcount < 10 · occupation cell < 3 · fewer than 2 employers per benchmark cell
Librarian ask in plain language

A natural-language surface over the lake. Ask in plain words and it extracts the concepts and geography against a controlled vocabulary, then routes to the datasets that answer, with live row counts. Deliberately a fake RAG: no embeddings and no generated answers, so a query can only ever point at datasets that exist. The catalog metadata is the index. Computation hands off to a separate analyst agent.

Deterministic routing over a 50-value concept vocabulary and 220 datasets, with zero model calls per query. The same routing decision is a visible concept match, not an opaque similarity score.
