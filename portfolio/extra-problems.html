Is our turnover bad, and against whom?

The peer benchmark

The problem. An employer's turnover number means nothing on its own. It only becomes a signal against comparable employers, at the occupation level, and that comparison is impossible while everyone names the same job differently.

The solve. Because every title resolves to a federal code, an employer sits against comparable employers at the same occupation. The bar is the peer range, the tick is the median, the dot is this firm. Surgical Technologist is the outlier worth a conversation. Medical Assistant is better than peers. Benchmarks are built under k-anonymity, so a thin cell is withheld rather than shown noisy.

Supply is abstract until it is a list of places to call

Where to hire from

The problem. A vacancy is only actionable if you know who nearby actually produces that worker. "Nurses are scarce" is a headline. "These four programs graduate 1,900 a year within an hour" is a plan.

Which moves from a job are actually worth backing?

Career transitions

The problem. Career advice is full of moves that sound plausible and fail in practice: a license wall, a wage cut, no local training. A worker or a workforce board needs the moves that clear all of those at once.

The solve. From any occupation, the model surfaces moves that raise the wage, need a credential or less, and already have a training pipeline nearby. Filled steps are recommendable now. Hollow steps are degree-gated and flagged as such, so nobody is sent down a two-year path dressed up as a lateral move.

What is an occupation actually made of?

The composition

The problem. To compare or reskill occupations you need to open them up. A job title is opaque. The skills and activities underneath it, weighted by how much they define the role, are what let you measure distance and exposure.

The solve. Each occupation is decomposed into its skills and work activities by learned weight. A filled bar is a defining component, the thing that makes the job what it is. A soft bar is contributing. Distance, overlap, and AI exposure are all read off this composition rather than off the title.

Occupations are not a list, they are a space

The skill space

The problem. A flat list of occupations hides the thing that matters most for mobility and shortage: which jobs are close to which. Closeness is not a taxonomy label, it is shared composition, and it has to be measured.

The solve. 798,000 occupation-pair similarities place every job in one space, where distance is shared composition. The clusters are emergent rather than assigned, and the same geometry feeds transitions, shortage substitution, and the adjacent-population supply. It is one object read many ways.

Stack Python · sentence-transformer embeddings · skill-proximity index · Supabase with pgvector
