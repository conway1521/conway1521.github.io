The occupation resolver

Employers name the same job a hundred ways: "AmbFloat LPN / II", "RN/Care Manager", "Sr. Scrum Master (Remote, LOCALS)". Nothing downstream, no benchmark, no forecast, no transition, works until those titles collapse onto one federal spine. This is how a messy title becomes a canonical name and a SOC-6 code, with certainty spent cheapest first and the unmatched flagged, never guessed.

Illustrative, synthetic throughout · real runs stay under tenant isolation
Resolve a title 10A · type or pick

Any employer title, two outputs: a canonical name a recruiter recognises, and the federal SOC-6 code. The margin says which model layer answered.

The three model layers 10B · called in sequence

Each layer's output routes onward only when confidence falls below its floor. The same stack runs on employer titles and public postings.

01Open-source NLP modelcandidate generation
A general-purpose sentence-transformer embeds the input title into a shared vector space with the full O*NET-SOC codebook and takes the nearest candidates. Handles the well-behaved majority for free.
sentence-transformers · MiniLM embeddings · cosine against the SOC codebook
02Fine-tuned NLP modelin-domain resolver
A domain-adapted encoder, fine-tuned on hundreds of thousands of employer-title-to-SOC pairs. Training labels generated by a locally-run LLM over normalised employer extracts, then reviewed.
encoder fine-tune · in-domain contrastive pairs · locally labelled at scale
03Local LLM verificationedge-case adjudication
A local LLM resolves cases where the upstream layers disagree or fall below their floors, and the long tail: novel titles, marketing-speak, compound roles. Output is JSON-constrained; inference is tenant-isolated.
Ollama · Qwen 2.5 · JSON-constrained output · tenant-isolated inference
