The data substrate

The lake everything else in this portfolio stands on: 220 datasets across twelve domains, reconciled onto one occupational spine, with standardisation treated as a first-class product rather than a chore.

Data engineering · data science — 220 datasets · 64 providers · 1.21M contributors · 8.9M records
01

The problem

Every analysis upstream of this page dies without one spine. Federal statistics, commercial postings, training records, credential rosters, and employer files disagree in unit, grain, vintage, and licence, and no two name an occupation the same way. Worse, the most valuable data, employers' own, can only join the pool if anonymity is engineered into the system rather than promised in a contract.

02

How it works

01One spine

A federal occupational taxonomy anchors everything; crosswalks bring industry, program, and skill classifications onto it; controlled vocabularies standardise everything ambiguous, termination reasons, employment status, facility types, so tenant data and public data speak one language.

Data engineering · data science

02The registry is the product

Every dataset carries tags for readiness, lifecycle stage, geographic and time grain, and provenance class. The catalog is queryable, versioned, and the single source of truth for what exists, at what quality, made from what.

Data engineering · product

03Contribution without exposure

Employer data joins under k-anonymity floors and row-level tenant isolation, with opaque identifiers throughout. The detailed panel is the source of truth; benchmarks are views over it, and a cell below its floor is withheld rather than shown.

Data engineering · privacy

04Kept honest by machines

Documentation drift is checked on every change; counts regenerate daily and commit themselves; phantom and orphan tables are audited automatically. The registry cannot quietly diverge from the database it describes.
