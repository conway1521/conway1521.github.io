Market signals

Forward-looking evidence on every state and occupation, gathered from fifteen distinct sources, held to tiered confidence rules, and composed into two flags whose logic anyone can read.

Economics · data science. Roughly 87,000 live signal observations feed every downstream surface.
01

The problem

Official statistics arrive quarters late. Decisions cannot wait for them. Leading evidence exists: postings, layoff notices, wage anomalies, federal awards, even the tone of regional economic reports. But it is noisy, uneven, and easy to over-read. The discipline is not in collecting more of it. It is in grading how much confidence each piece deserves and composing it so the logic stays readable.

02

How it works

01Fifteen evidence types, one grain

Every signal, quantitative or qualitative, lands on the same state-by-occupation grain, so a wage anomaly, a retirement wave, and a sentiment shift can be read against each other rather than in separate reports.

Data science · engineering

02Tiered confidence

Strong sources fire alone. Weaker ones only count when two agree in the same direction. Unconfirmed signals are kept and labelled rather than dropped, because a flagged rumour that later confirms is itself information.

Economics · data science

03Two flags, legible composition

Everything rolls up to two flags, market stress and rising market, each an explicit OR over named signals. A reader can always answer "why is this flag up" by naming the signal that raised it.

Economics

04Language as a data source

Qualitative sources (regional economic commentary, trade press) are read by an LLM under a constrained extraction schema, turning prose into the same signal grain as the statistics, at negligible cost per release.

Final state

The system runs live across all fifty states, roughly 87,000 signal observations over a larger base of source rows, refreshed on each source's native cadence, and feeds the opportunity index, the state dashboards, and the labour market twin. The signal types and confidence rules shown are the production rules. The example notes are illustrative.
