Labor market digital twin

A state cannot plan on statistics that lag by years and projections it cannot open. The twin does the accounting instead: five inflows, three outflows, demand built four ways, and the gap is what the model solves for.

Economics · policy · data science · engineering. First end-to-end run projects a state nursing workforce to 2033 and is informing state policy.
01

The problem

States plan their workforce with statistics that lag by years and live in silos: employment here, graduations there, migration somewhere else, none of them in the same accounting frame. Projections arrive as black boxes, a number with no visible mechanics. The alternative is to model the market the way an economist would audit it: as stocks and flows that must balance, with every term measured, sourced, and carrying its uncertainty.

02

How it works

01Supply as conserved flows

Cohort accounting over 501 occupations and 51 jurisdictions: graduates in from the training pipeline, migration in and out from nineteen vintages of census microdata, exits and retirements from empirical age-specific rates. The identity must hold, so nothing can be fudged.

Economics · data science

02Demand built four ways, never averaged

Requisition signals, structural projections, population-anchored forward need, and facility blueprints: four estimates of the same demand, kept apart on purpose. Where they agree, act. Where they diverge, investigate. Divergence is information, not error.

Economics · data science

03Sourced terms, flagged gaps

Transfers are removed from exits before anything is projected. Thin cells fall back to national rates and say so. Harmonizing two decades of occupational codes carries uncertainty flags rather than silent joins.

Data science · engineering

04From projection to decision

On top of the engine: transition pathways gated by credential requirements and wage premiums, forward-looking market signals, and evidence-graded policy levers, so a projected gap comes with the moves that could close it.

Adjacent population hover an occupation

Supply into an occupation is not only the people already in it. It is everyone one retraining step away. For a target occupation the twin spells out that reachable pool: which occupations are skill-adjacent, how large each pool is, and what bridge it takes to cross.

Each occupation around the target is one retraining step away. Near steps are drawn solid, mid dashed, far dotted. Hover an occupation for its pool, its bridge, and the wage change.
Adjacency from 798k occupation-pair similarities, tiered by retraining distance. The reachable pool is what the model feeds into the adjacent-population inflow, discounted by the credential and wage distance of each bridge.

Demand is estimated four separate ways, each catching a different signal at a different horizon. They are never averaged, because where the four agree that agreement is itself the confidence, and where they diverge the divergence is a read on which horizon is moving.

04

Final state

The structure is the production system: the same flows, identities, and demand layers run over real census microdata, training-pipeline records, and employer panels. The first end-to-end run projects one state's nursing workforce through 2033 across all of its counties, and is informing state workforce policy and training investment. Where the engine can be checked against the federal system it is: employer-survey forward demand for the pilot occupation lands within 7% of the federal projection. The curves and magnitudes shown here are illustrative. The real runs carry their sources and uncertainty with them.
