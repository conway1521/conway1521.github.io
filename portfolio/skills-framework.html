Portfolio Calibrated · every parameter sourced

The task-based labour market

A calibrated production function that reads AI exposure at the task level: what happens to the skill mix when an AI capability lands, measured as an instrument rather than argued as an estimate.

Economics · machine learning. Engine built, outputs cached at scale. Companion paper in progress.
01

The problem

Seven significant measures of AI exposure now exist, and they disagree: the pre-LLM measures correlate negatively with the post-LLM ones. Meanwhile claims about AI's effect on wages and employment outrun anyone's ability to measure them. What the debate needs is not another estimate but an instrument: a framework whose every parameter is disclosed, sourced, and swappable, so that when results differ you can see exactly which assumption made them differ.

02

How it works

01The economy as tasks

Three nested levels: an industry combines tasks in stable expenditure shares. Each task is produced from capital and labour with a task-specific substitution elasticity. The labour input is itself a bundle of skills. Automation-resistant tasks and exposed tasks differ in one parameter, not in kind.

Economics

02Calibrated, never estimated

No elasticity is estimated from market data and no equilibrium is solved. Every parameter is calibrated to a published source, staffing matrices, task-importance profiles, output weights, skill proximities, and carries its provenance. Defensibility is the traceability.

Economics · data science

03Exposure as a pluggable adapter

The AI-exposure parameters are read from interchangeable adapters implementing one contract, several published measures wired behind the same interface. Robustness across adapters is a built-in output, converting the field's fragmentation from a liability into a feature.

Machine learning · engineering

04Validated against observed shifts

The headline test is predictive: does calibrated task-level exposure anticipate observed wage shifts better than flat per-occupation scores and a zero-information null? Validation runs on healthcare occupations, where within-industry variation in substitutability is largest.

Econometrics
The framework three levels, two outputs

Cobb-Douglas over tasks. Capital against labour within each task. Skills within labour. The two engine outputs follow directly.

Industry combines tasks
Y = A · Πi Tiβi
Each industry produces output by combining tasks in stable expenditure shares βi.
Each task combines capital and labour
Ti = Bi · [ δi Kiρi + (1−δi) Liρi ]1/ρi
The substitution elasticity σi = 1/(1−ρi) is task-specific: near zero, capital and labour are complements and the task resists automation. Large, they substitute and the task is exposed.
Labour is a bundle of skills
Li = CES( S1 … SK . γi,k, σj,k )
Skill shares γi,k are task-specific. Pairwise elasticities σj,k say which skills substitute within a task and which cannot.
The outputs
θk = Σi βi (1−δi) γi,k     ∂θk/∂δi = −βi γi,k
θk is skill k's weight in aggregate output. Its derivative is skill k's exposure to a marginal increase in task i's capital intensity. Every surface and every figure reads from these two.
Not a structural model: no elasticities estimated, no equilibrium solved, no causal claims about wages or employment. A measurement instrument with disclosed parameters.
Where every parameter comes from the provenance

None of the parameters are estimated structurally. Each is traceable to a published source.

βi · industry task shares
National staffing matrices × task importance profiles, weighted by industry output.
δi, σi · capital share and substitutability
Pluggable AI-exposure adapters: several published task-level measures behind one contract, with a blended option.
γi,k · skill shares within a task
Task-to-skill importance matrices, with an embedding fallback where no direct mapping exists.
σj,k · skill-to-skill substitutability
Occupation-pair skill proximity, a monotone transform of cosine similarity over skills, knowledge, and work activities.
Every engine output carries its adapter and calibration vintage as columns. No anonymous numbers.
03

Final state

The engine is built and its outputs are cached at scale: skill exposure, role resilience, and a task atlas of several hundred thousand calibrated rows, each carrying its adapter and calibration vintage. The framework doubles as a research instrument: a companion paper on measuring labour-market resilience and AI exposure is in progress. The equations above are the production system. Worked magnitudes are deliberately not shown here.
