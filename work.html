Work

I built and run Radius Intelligence (RI)'s data department. The platform reconciles every input the labour market produces: federal labour and demographic statistics, commercial postings, training and credential records, fiscal and academic signals, and anonymised employer data from RI Technologies clients. Alongside the training, credential, and staffing inventories already in the lake, the result is an end-to-end view of the labour market: who supplies workers, who employs them, who certifies them, and who staffs the gaps. It covers every occupation across the US at every depth of geography, and grows every time a new employer connects.

I built Radius's data department and run it end to end, across four disciplines:

  • Economics. Regional economic analysis and labour market forecasting at the core, designed and grounded in macroeconomic theory. The methods produce both explanations and policy levers.
  • Engineering and architecture. A compliant, layered data intelligence layer with Supabase-backed tenant isolation, that reconciles federal, commercial, and academic sources into unique-in-the-field combinations that actually guide practitioners.
  • Data science. Modelling workforce and labour market dynamics: tenure survival, demand forecasting, occupational similarity, skill clustering, AI substitution exposure, and task-skill production functions.
  • Product engineering. The software and AI surfaces on top of the data work: client-facing forecasting pipelines, a Lake Browser, a concept-routing dataset finder, and an analyst copilot for natural-language exploration. Turns the lake into decisions stakeholders can actually act on.

RI Technologies handles client-based employer forecasting and workforce planning. RI Labs covers labour market analysis and policy innovation. The data substrate itself stands as a product.
