Work

I built and run Radius Intelligence (RI)'s data department: a platform that reconciles federal statistics, commercial postings, training and credential records, fiscal signals, and anonymised employer data from clients into one view across every US occupation and geography. The labour market from every side: who supplies workers, who employs them, who certifies them, and who staffs the gaps. The dataset grows every time a new client connects.

Current

RI Technologies handles client-side employer workforce planning: HRIS standardisation, vacancy and fill-rate diagnostics, turnover and demand forecasting with scenario modelling, and prescriptive guidance through training and staffing inventories. An AI analyst agent runs across the workflow.

RI Labs handles labour market and policy analysis: occupation scoring on demand, mobility, growth, automation durability, and job quality; skill-based transition pathways; market signals from 15 evidence types across every US state and occupation; synthesis reports on regional conditions, sector health, and supply-demand imbalances.

Both run on a compliant multi-tenant Supabase data layer that reconciles federal statistics, commercial sources, training outcomes, employer HRIS, federal spending, and academic data across every US occupation and geography, standardised through a SOC pipeline of regex, embedding matching, and LLM fallback, with transformer fine-tuning for occupational and skills intelligence.

Underpinned by flow-based labour market economics: stock-flow decomposition, search and matching frictions, cohort projection, micro-to-macro. Explicit econometrics (survival analysis, ARIMA/SARIMA, network econometrics) over black-box ML. Building toward a full digital twin of the US labour market: employer benchmarks, training outcomes, migration, retirement pressure, skills evolution, AI substitution exposure.
